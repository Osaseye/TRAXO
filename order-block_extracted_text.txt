1. Introduction & Origins

1.1 What Is the Order Block Strategy?

The Order Block (OB) strategy is one of the most powerful concepts within the Smart Money Concepts (SMC) and ICT (Inner Circle Trader) trading methodology. At its core, an order block represents a specific price zone on a chart where large institutional traders — banks, hedge funds, central banks, and prime brokers — have executed massive buy or sell orders before a significant market move.

Unlike traditional support and resistance concepts that rely solely on historical price reactions, order blocks identify the exact mechanics of how institutions execute trades. Because institutions deal in billions of dollars of capital, they cannot fill their entire position in a single moment. Instead, they distribute their orders across a range of prices — and these zones leave a visible footprint on the chart.

When price retraces to these footprints, the remaining unfilled institutional orders create high-probability reaction points, allowing retail traders to align with the dominant market force rather than trade against it.

1.2 History & Creator

The Order Block concept was developed and popularized by Michael Huddleston, widely known online as "The Inner Circle Trader" (ICT). A self-taught American trader and analyst, Huddleston built his methodology over decades of studying institutional trading behavior, central bank mechanics, and the Interbank Price Delivery Algorithm (IPDA) — the algorithmic framework used by major institutions to deliver price from one liquidity pool to another.

Huddleston began publishing his concepts publicly via YouTube and mentorship programs, creating a framework that teaches retail traders to "see the market as institutions do." Order Blocks are among the foundational elements of this framework, alongside Fair Value Gaps (FVG), Liquidity Sweeps, Market Structure Shifts (MSS), and the Optimal Trade Entry (OTE) concept.

NOTE

Order Blocks are not a standalone indicator or mechanical signal. They are a lens for understanding institutional intent, and must be combined with market structure analysis, session timing, and multi-timeframe confluence to achieve high probability.

1.3 Why Order Blocks Work

Order Blocks work because of a fundamental asymmetry in the market: institutions cannot move large positions instantly without causing massive slippage. To accumulate or distribute positions efficiently, they must operate across multiple candles and price levels. This process of "layering" orders creates identifiable zones where:

Unfilled limit orders remain waiting to be executed on a retrace

Institutional algorithms are programmed to re-engage at these zones

Price action shows predictable reactions when these zones are tapped

Retail stop-loss clusters are strategically targeted before these reactions

The result is a self-fulfilling prophecy reinforced by both fundamental institutional behavior and algorithmic order management systems. This is why Order Blocks show consistent respect across all markets and all timeframes.

2. Core Concepts & Prerequisites

2.1 Market Structure Fundamentals

Before identifying Order Blocks, a trader must understand the underlying market structure that gives them context and validity. Order Blocks without structural context are meaningless.

Break of Structure (BOS)

A Break of Structure occurs when price creates a new high above the previous swing high in a bullish trend, or a new low below the previous swing low in a bearish trend. A BOS confirms trend continuation and is the primary signal that an Order Block has "launched" and is now valid for future retests.

Change of Character (CHoCH)

A Change of Character is a more significant structural shift — it occurs when price breaks in the opposite direction of the prevailing trend for the first time, signaling a potential trend reversal. A CHoCH is more powerful than a BOS because it represents a genuine shift in institutional intent rather than simple continuation.

Liquidity (Buy-Side & Sell-Side)

Liquidity refers to clusters of orders (stop-losses, pending orders) that exist at predictable levels — swing highs (buy-side liquidity / BSL) and swing lows (sell-side liquidity / SSL). Institutions target these liquidity pools to fill their large orders at favorable prices before reversing direction. Understanding where liquidity sits is essential to predicting where Order Blocks will form and how they will be used.

Premium & Discount Arrays

Institutional traders prefer to buy in discount (below the 50% equilibrium of a price range) and sell in premium (above the 50% equilibrium). Order Blocks that sit in discount zones are stronger bullish setups; Order Blocks in premium zones carry stronger bearish intent.

Fair Value Gap (FVG)

A Fair Value Gap is a three-candle pattern where a gap exists between the high of candle 1 and the low of candle 3, created by the momentum candle in the middle. FVGs often appear alongside Order Blocks and serve as additional confluence zones for entry precision.

2.2 The Interbank Price Delivery Algorithm (IPDA)

The IPDA is the institutional algorithmic framework that delivers price from one liquidity pool to the next in an organized, logical sequence. Understanding IPDA means understanding that markets are not random — they follow a systematic process of accumulation, manipulation, and distribution (AMD). Order Blocks are the "staging areas" within this algorithmic delivery system.

KEY INSIGHT

Price always moves from one pool of liquidity to another. Order Blocks mark the zones where this algorithmic delivery pauses, accumulates, or distributes before the next move. Trading OBs means trading WITH the algorithm, not against it.

3. Types of Order Blocks

3.1 Bullish Order Block (BOB)

A Bullish Order Block is the last bearish (red/down) candle — or the last down-closing candle — immediately before a significant bullish impulse move that creates a Break of Structure to the upside. It represents the zone where institutional traders placed their large buy orders, disguising their accumulation as a bearish candle to attract retail sellers.

Characteristics of a valid Bullish Order Block:

It is the LAST bearish candle before the bullish impulse begins

The following bullish candle engulfs it body-to-body and wick-to-wick

The impulse creates a Fair Value Gap (imbalance) to the upside

A Break of Structure or Market Structure Shift (MSS) is confirmed

The zone is defined by the Open and Close of the bearish candle

Sits in a discount zone (below 50% equilibrium) for highest probability

Figure 1: Bullish Order Block — anatomy showing the bearish candle (OB), the bullish impulse, the OB zone, and the retest entry with Stop Loss and Take Profit levels.

3.2 Bearish Order Block (BeOB)

A Bearish Order Block is the last bullish (green/up) candle — or the last up-closing candle — immediately before a significant bearish impulse move that creates a Break of Structure to the downside. It marks where institutions placed their large sell orders, disguised as bullish momentum to attract retail buyers before the reversal.

Characteristics of a valid Bearish Order Block:

It is the LAST bullish candle before the bearish impulse begins

The following bearish candle engulfs it fully (body and wick)

The impulse leaves a Fair Value Gap (imbalance) to the downside

A Break of Structure or Market Structure Shift (MSS) is confirmed downside

The zone is defined by the Open and Close of the bullish candle

Sits in a premium zone (above 50% equilibrium) for highest probability

Figure 2: Bearish Order Block — showing the bullish candle (OB), the bearish impulse, the OB zone, and the retest entry with Stop Loss above and Take Profit below.

3.3 Breaker Block

A Breaker Block is a failed Order Block — an Order Block that price has broken through completely rather than respecting. Once an OB fails, it flips its polarity: a failed Bullish OB becomes a bearish Breaker Block (acting as resistance), and a failed Bearish OB becomes a bullish Breaker Block (acting as support).

Formation process of a Breaker Block:

An Order Block forms and price initially respects it

Price returns and breaks THROUGH the Order Block (failure)

A Change of Character (CHoCH) confirms the structural shift

The broken OB zone is now the Breaker Block

Price retests the Breaker from the other side — this is the entry

Breaker Blocks are reversal tools — the strongest OB-family signal for trend change trades, particularly effective after liquidity sweeps.

Figure 3: Breaker Block — a Bullish OB fails, flips to resistance (Bearish Breaker Block), and price retests the level for a short entry.

3.4 Mitigation Block

A Mitigation Block is an old Order Block that has been partially mitigated (tapped) and is being revisited again. Unlike a Breaker Block (which flips direction), a Mitigation Block continues to act in its original direction, but with reduced strength because it has already been partially consumed. They represent zones where institutional algorithms return to re-execute pending orders that were not fully filled on the first visit.

3.5 Rejection Block

A Rejection Block is formed at the wick of a large candlestick at a market high or low — typically visible as a long upper or lower shadow. It occurs after a liquidity sweep (false breakout) where price spikes through equal highs or equal lows, triggers retail stop orders, and then sharply reverses. Rejection Blocks are characterized by:

Long wicks with little to no body — price was rejected immediately

Formation at previous swing highs/lows or equal high/low clusters

Strong volume during the wick (evidence of institutional absorption)

Immediate reversal after the sweep — confirmation of the manipulation

4.2 Step-by-Step Identification Process

Identify the higher-timeframe trend direction (Daily/4H) — this is your bias.

Look for consolidation areas or ranges where price has been accumulating or distributing.

Identify the strong impulse move that broke out of the range — the one that created the BOS.

Find the LAST candle in the opposite direction of the impulse, immediately before it began. This is your candidate OB.

Confirm C1–C4: engulf, MSS, FVG, structural origin. If all four are met: valid OB.

Mark the zone from the OPEN to the CLOSE of the OB candle (some traders extend to the full High–Low).

Wait. Do NOT enter on the impulse. Wait for price to retrace back into the OB zone.

On retest, drop to a lower timeframe (LTF) for entry confirmation: look for MSS, FVG fill, or rejection wick at the zone.

5. The Institutional Cycle — AMD Framework

Order Blocks do not exist in isolation. They form at specific, predictable points within the institutional trading cycle known as Accumulation, Manipulation, and Distribution (AMD). Understanding this cycle is essential to knowing WHEN an Order Block is about to become tradeable.

Figure 4: The AMD Cycle — Accumulation (smart money builds position), Manipulation (liquidity sweep trapping retail), Distribution (price moves in the real institutional direction). Order Blocks form at the manipulation phase.

5.1 Accumulation Phase

During accumulation, smart money builds their position gradually within a defined range. Price moves sideways with no clear trend, creating confusion among retail traders. This phase is characterized by equal highs and equal lows (liquidity pools forming), tight consolidation with no significant breakout, and growing volume with limited price movement.

5.2 Manipulation Phase (Where OBs Form)

This is the most critical phase for Order Block traders. Institutions deliberately push price in the OPPOSITE direction of their intended trade to:

Trigger retail stop-losses below support (for bullish setups) or above resistance (for bearish setups)

Collect the liquidity generated by those stop orders to fill their large positions

Create a convincing false breakout that traps retail traders on the wrong side

The ORDER BLOCK forms at the candle immediately before the manipulation completes and the real move begins. This is why the last candle before the impulse is so critical — it is the exact moment institutions filled their orders and reversed direction.

5.3 Distribution Phase

After manipulation, the institutional position is fully built. Price now moves decisively in the real direction — the distribution phase. This is the trend everyone should have been trading. Retail traders who are now trapped on the wrong side are forced to exit at a loss, adding fuel to the institutional move.

EXAMPLE

EUR/USD consolidates for 6 hours (Accumulation). In the Asian session, price breaks below equal lows triggering retail sell stops (Manipulation). The last green candle before this drop = Bearish OB. Price then rockets up in the London session for 150 pips (Distribution). Traders who identified the Bearish OB zone entered short on the retest for a clean trade.

6.2 Processing Logic — Pseudocode

FUNCTION identify_order_block(candles[], timeframe, session):

  htf_bias = get_htf_trend(daily_chart, weekly_chart)

  IF htf_bias == NEUTRAL: return NULL  // No trade — no clear bias

  FOR each candle[i] in candles[]:

    IF htf_bias == BULLISH:

      IF candle[i].color == RED AND candle[i+1].color == GREEN:

        impulse = candle[i+1]

        IF impulse.close > previous_swing_high:  // BOS confirmed

          IF impulse.low < candle[i].low AND impulse.high > candle[i].high:  // Engulf

            IF has_fvg(candle[i], candle[i+1], candle[i+2]):  // Imbalance

              OB_zone = {low: candle[i].close, high: candle[i].open}

              OB_quality = grade_ob(OB_zone, session, volume, htf_level)

              RETURN OB_zone, OB_quality

    IF htf_bias == BEARISH:

      IF candle[i].color == GREEN AND candle[i+1].color == RED:

        impulse = candle[i+1]

        IF impulse.close < previous_swing_low:  // BOS confirmed

          IF impulse engulfs candle[i]:  // Body + wick

            IF has_fvg(candle[i], candle[i+1], candle[i+2]):

              OB_zone = {low: candle[i].open, high: candle[i].close}

              OB_quality = grade_ob(OB_zone, session, volume, htf_level)

              RETURN OB_zone, OB_quality

  RETURN NULL  // No valid OB found

7. Entry Techniques

There are three primary entry methods for Order Block trades, ranging from aggressive to conservative. The right choice depends on your risk tolerance and the quality of the OB setup.

7.1 Method 1 — Limit Order Entry (Conservative)

Place a limit order at the 50% mark (equilibrium) of the Order Block zone before price arrives. This is the Optimal Trade Entry (OTE) technique and requires pre-planning.

Enter: Limit buy/sell at the 50% retracement of the OB zone (open + close / 2)

Advantage: Best average price; widest room before invalidation

Disadvantage: OB may not be reached; requires patience and pre-identification

Best for: Swing traders and day traders working from higher timeframes

7.2 Method 2 — Market Structure Shift Entry (Standard)

Wait for price to tap the OB zone, then drop to a lower timeframe and wait for a confirmed Market Structure Shift in the direction of the OB before entering.

Enter: On the LTF close of the MSS confirmation candle

Advantage: Highest confirmation; dramatically reduces false entries

Disadvantage: Later entry means slightly wider stop; may miss fast moves

Best for: Day traders using 15M/5M confirmation on 1H/4H OBs

7.3 Method 3 — Aggressive Entry (For Scalpers)

8. Risk Management

8.1 Stop Loss Placement

Stop loss placement is mechanical with Order Blocks — there is a defined invalidation level that tells you exactly when the trade idea is wrong:

Bullish OB: Stop Loss placed 10–20 pips (or 0.1–0.2 ATR) BELOW the LOW of the Order Block candle

Bearish OB: Stop Loss placed 10–20 pips (or 0.1–0.2 ATR) ABOVE the HIGH of the Order Block candle

Hard invalidation: If price CLOSES beyond the full wick of the OB candle on the same timeframe, the OB is invalid — no trade should be taken

The buffer (10–20 pips) accounts for stop-loss hunting (deliberate spike through the OB to clear retail stops before the real move). Without a buffer, you will be stopped out by normal volatility around the zone.

8.2 Take Profit Targets

Take profit targets are set based on the next logical draw on liquidity — where price is algorithmically programmed to travel next:

TP1 (Partial — 50% of position): Previous swing high/low in the trade direction, or the nearest Fair Value Gap

TP2 (Partial — 25% of position): Next untouched Order Block in the trade direction

TP3 (Remaining 25%): Major buy-side or sell-side liquidity pool (equal highs/lows on HTF)

A minimum 1:2 Risk:Reward ratio is required for any OB trade. Quality setups on Daily/4H timeframes commonly achieve 1:5 to 1:15 RR.

8.3 Position Sizing

Use the following formula for position sizing regardless of market or timeframe:

Position Size = (Account Balance x Risk %) / (Entry Price - Stop Loss Price)

Maximum risk per trade: 1%–2% of total account balance

Never exceed 5% total exposure across all open OB trades simultaneously

Scale position size down on lower-quality OBs (second tap, counter-trend, Asian session)

8.4 Trade Invalidation Rules

An Order Block trade must be immediately closed or the pending order cancelled if:

Price closes beyond the full High-Low range of the OB candle on the trading timeframe

A CHoCH occurs in the OPPOSITE direction before price reaches the OB

A major news event creates a gap beyond the OB zone

The OB has been tapped more than twice (significantly weakened)

9. Multi-Timeframe Analysis (MTF)

Multi-timeframe analysis is not optional with the Order Block strategy — it is fundamental to it. The only OBs that achieve consistently high win rates are those that are aligned across at least two timeframes.

Figure 6: Multi-Timeframe Analysis — Daily chart establishes bias, 4H locates the retest zone, 15M provides the precise entry trigger.

9.1 The Top-Down Approach

Always analyze from the highest timeframe down to the entry timeframe:

MONTHLY / WEEKLY: Identify the macro trend direction and major institutional levels (major highs, lows, OBs from months prior). This is your master bias.

DAILY: Identify the intermediate trend, key OB zones for the week, and the directional bias for the next 3–5 days. Mark the Daily OBs that are fresh and relevant.

4-HOUR: Identify the current phase (AMD cycle stage), the intermediate OBs, and whether price is approaching a Daily OB for a retest. The 4H is your "setup" timeframe.

1-HOUR / 15-MINUTE: The entry timeframe — confirm a Market Structure Shift at the OB zone, look for FVG fill, and identify the specific entry trigger candle.

5-MINUTE / 1-MINUTE (for scalpers): Ultra-precise entry timing — look for the first strong displacement candle in the OB direction to pull the trigger.

10.1 Scalping (1M–5M)

Order Blocks on the 1-minute and 3-minute timeframes exist but are extremely short-lived — typically lasting minutes to hours. They are highly susceptible to noise and require MANDATORY higher-timeframe confluence (minimum 15M or 1H OB in same direction) to have any probabilistic edge. Risk:Reward is limited (1:1.5 to 1:3) but trade frequency is very high.

Best instruments: Highly liquid markets — EUR/USD, Gold (XAU/USD), Bitcoin, NQ/ES futures

Minimum confluence: 15M or higher OB alignment + session timing

10.2 Day Trading (15M–1H)

The 15-minute and 1-hour timeframes are the most commonly used entry timeframes for day traders using the OB strategy. They provide a balance between trade frequency and signal quality. Typical hold times of 2–8 hours allow for clear OB formations and meaningful RR targets.

HTF context required: 4H or Daily OB as the anchor zone

Entry trigger: 15M MSS or FVG fill at the 4H/1H OB

Session focus: London Open and NY AM sessions exclusively

10.3 Swing Trading (4H–Daily)

The 4-hour and Daily timeframes produce the most reliable Order Blocks due to the concentration of institutional activity. Swing trades last from days to weeks and offer the best Risk:Reward ratios (1:5 to 1:15). These timeframes reduce noise dramatically and require fewer trades for equivalent returns.

HTF context: Weekly OB alignment for Daily trades; Daily for 4H trades

Entry trigger: 1H or 4H MSS at the Daily OB; patience is critical

Best markets: Forex majors, Gold, S&P 500, NQ, Bitcoin

10.4 Position Trading (Weekly–Monthly)

Monthly and Weekly Order Blocks represent the most powerful institutional footprints on any chart — zones where major accumulation or distribution has occurred over months. These OBs can hold for years and generate extraordinary RR when correctly identified. Position trading with OBs requires extreme patience and is typically reserved for experienced traders.

TIMEFRAME RULE

The higher the timeframe of the Order Block, the more powerful and long-lasting it is. A Daily OB will override a 15M OB. A Weekly OB will override a Daily OB. ALWAYS trade in the direction of the highest available OB.

12. Advanced Confluences & Combinations

Order Blocks become significantly more powerful when combined with other institutional concepts. The more confluence factors align, the higher the probability of a successful trade.

12.1 OB + Fair Value Gap (FVG) Strategy

When a Fair Value Gap exists within or adjacent to an Order Block zone, the probability of a strong reaction increases dramatically. This is because institutions have two reasons to defend the zone: the OB orders AND the FVG imbalance. Entry strategy: Wait for price to retrace into the OB zone, watch for the FVG within the OB to be filled, then enter on the first rejection candle. Stop Loss sits below the OB low (bullish) or above OB high (bearish).

12.2 OB + Liquidity Sweep Strategy

The highest probability OB setups occur AFTER a liquidity sweep. The sequence is: (1) Price sweeps a significant high or low (the liquidity grab), (2) The last candle before the sweep reversal is the OB, (3) Price returns to the OB zone for retest and continuation. This sequence confirms that institutions have completed their order-filling process and are ready to drive price in the intended direction.

12.3 OB + Fibonacci Confluence

The Optimal Trade Entry (OTE) zone (62%–79% Fibonacci retracement) frequently aligns with Order Block zones. When the 62%–79% Fibonacci retracement of a swing move falls within an Order Block, it creates an exceptionally powerful entry zone that is used by institutional algorithms as the ideal position entry.

12.4 OB + Market Structure (BOS/CHoCH)

An Order Block that is directly responsible for creating a Break of Structure (BOS) or Change of Character (CHoCH) carries the highest institutional weight of any OB type. These are called "High Probability Order Blocks" or "Source OBs" — they are the exact zones where the institutional trend was established.

CONFLUENCE RULE

Score your setups: +1 for each confluence factor (HTF alignment, fresh OB, liquidity sweep prior, FVG present, session timing, Fibonacci OTE alignment). Only take trades scoring 4 or higher out of 6. This filter alone dramatically improves win rate.
