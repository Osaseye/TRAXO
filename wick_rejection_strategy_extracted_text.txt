Introduction — What is Wick Rejection?The Wick Rejection Strategy is one of the most widely used price action techniques in modern trading. It is built on the principle that when price violently tests a level and is pushed back, that violent rejection leaves a visible scar on the chart in the form of a long candlestick wick. This wick is not just a visual artifact — it is a direct representation of a battle between buyers and sellers, with one side overwhelming the other and snapping price back in the opposite direction.At its core, wick rejection trading is the art of reading what markets are communicating through these wicks: where strong hands entered, where weak hands were trapped, and where the next directional move is likely to originate. When combined with context — key levels, trend alignment, volume, and confirmation — wick rejection setups become some of the highest probability entries available in any market.KEY DEFINITIONA wick rejection occurs when the market price aggressively moves into a key support or resistance level, but buyers or sellers overwhelm the move, causing price to snap back and the candle to close far from the extreme — leaving behind a long wick (shadow) as evidence of that rejection.This document covers the complete framework: the anatomy of the signal, the psychology behind it, the algorithmic logic required to identify and trade it systematically, risk management rules, market-specific applications, timeframe guidelines, and common pitfalls to avoid.2. It tells you exactly how far price traveled in a direction before the opposing force became too strong. A long wick means:Price ventured deep into a zone (often a key S/R level, liquidity pool, or psychological number)Sellers (for upper wick) or buyers (for lower wick) entered aggressivelyThe initial push was completely overpowered — the close snapped back significantlyTrapped traders are now on the wrong side and will fuel the move furtherThink of a wick as a failed attempt. The market tried to go somewhere, failed, and the remnants of that failure are visible as a long wick on the chart.3. Market Psychology Behind Wick RejectionUnderstanding why wick rejection works requires understanding what happens at the participant level during the formation of these candles. Markets are driven by human (and algorithmic) behavior, and wick rejections are the fingerprints of institutional order flow.3.1 The Institutional FootprintLarge institutions — hedge funds, banks, and algorithmic trading systems — cannot enter positions instantly in large size without moving the market against themselves. To solve this, they use liquidity engineering: they deliberately drive price into zones where retail traders have placed stop-loss orders or where breakout traders will pile in. This creates the liquidity pool they need to fill large orders at favorable prices.HOW IT WORKS1. Retail longs place stops below support. Retail shorts enter breakouts below support. 2. Institutions drive price below support — triggering all those stops and attracting new sellers. 3. Institutions absorb ALL that selling to accumulate long positions. 4. Price snaps back above support immediately — leaving a long lower wick. 5. All the breakout sellers are now trapped. Their panic buying fuels the rally.3.2 The Psychology of Trapped TradersWhen a wick rejection forms, there are always two groups of traders who got caught:Trader TypeTheir Situation After the Wick RejectionBreakout Traders (Long Wick)They entered in the direction of the wick, expecting a breakout. The wick snapped back against them. They are now holding losing positions and will eventually cut losses — adding fuel to the reversal.Stop-Hunted TradersTheir stops were triggered by the wick. They were shaken out of profitable positions. They may re-enter in the original direction once price recovers, fueling more momentum.Late-Entry Trend TradersEntered just before the wick formed, riding what looked like momentum. Now trapped and underwater, forced to close positions as price moves against them.The combined forced exit of all these trapped traders is what gives wick rejection moves their explosive character. You are not just following a signal — you are riding the forced liquidations of everyone on the wrong side.3.3 The Liquidity Sweep MechanismThe modern understanding of wick rejection is inseparable from the concept of a liquidity sweep. Price does not randomly reject from levels — it is deliberately driven to those levels to harvest the liquidity (orders) sitting there before the true directional move begins.A liquidity sweep looks like a false breakout. Price pierces a key level, wicks dramatically into or through it, then reverses with force. This is the institution's entry point, and the long wick is the evidence of their presence. Trading the wick rejection after a liquidity sweep means aligning yourself with institutional order flow — the most powerful force in any market.4. When price returns to this zone in the future, it should be treated as a high-probability long entry — institutions are re-entering the same position they built during the original sweep.5. Identification Criteria — Step-by-StepA valid wick rejection setup requires multiple conditions to align simultaneously. Meeting one or two conditions is not sufficient. The more conditions that align, the higher the probability.6.1 Primary Conditions (All Required)The wick-to-body ratio is at minimum 2:1 (ideally 3:1 or greater).The wick is touching or penetrating a key support or resistance level, supply/demand zone, previous swing high/low, or psychological price level.The candle body closes significantly away from the wick extreme — returning back into the prior range or above/below a key level.The wick is pointing in the direction of the level being tested (lower wick at support, upper wick at resistance).At least one higher timeframe is in agreement with the trade direction (HTF bias filter).6.2 Secondary Conditions (Confluence Boosters)Volume is elevated on the rejection candle compared to the average of the prior 10–20 candles.The rejection occurs after a clear prior trend in the direction of the wick (a downtrend into support for a bullish wick).A Fair Value Gap (FVG) or imbalance is present near the rejection zone.Fibonacci retracement level (0.618, 0.786) coincides with the rejection zone.A moving average (20 EMA, 50 EMA, 200 EMA) acts as additional support/resistance at the wick level.The session timing is optimal (London open, New York open — not illiquid overnight sessions).Round number or psychological level (e.g., 1.1000 in EUR/USD, $50,000 in Bitcoin) coincides with the wick.The wick follows a liquidity sweep of obvious retail stops (equal highs/lows, previous daily high/low).6.3 Disqualifying Conditions (Do NOT Trade)The wick forms in the middle of a range with no nearby key level.Market is in a tight sideways chop — wicks form everywhere and mean nothing.Session is illiquid (e.g., 3:00–6:00 AM GMT for Forex — dead hours).Major news event is imminent within 15–30 minutes (pre-news wicks are manipulated and unpredictable).The wick-to-body ratio is below 1.5:1.The higher timeframe trend is strongly opposed to your trade direction with no clear structure shift.Price has recently repeatedly tested and failed to hold the same level (worn out level).7.
